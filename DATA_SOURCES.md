# Data sources

Version 1.0.0, 24 November 2026. What the three scenarios say about the world is real, checked at source and listed here with the date it was read. The companies are fictional and their own figures illustrative: every screen says "Fictional company", and the Start step says that its figures are made up. The scenario checker (`tools/check-scenarios.mjs`) fails when a scenario cites a source this file does not list with its date.

## 1. Real and fictional

| Part | Status |
|---|---|
| The three companies, their names, staff, plants, products and own figures | Fictional, invented to be plausible for their size |
| What the team entered, what the agents wrote, the euro ranges, weights and ratings | Authored by hand as a recorded run would return them; the euro ranges, weights and ratings are AI estimates in the story, structured judgements, not measurements |
| Laws, rules and market facts the agents cite | Real, from the sources below, with the date read |
| The methods: the factor tables, the IE matrix and the TOWS matrix | Real, from the sources in section 5 |

A scenario older than 90 days shows "Check for updates" on the SIMULATION tape.

Auental's town is set as a middle-order town, so its heat plan is due by the end of this year.

### Aligned with the brief in 1.0.0

The independent review of 24 November 2026 found two places where a scenario's own description strayed from the brief. Tidewell Lines was based in Hamburg; the brief and the sister tools place it in Bremen, and so does it now. Auental Energie supplied power, gas and water; the brief adds heat, the local grid and car charging, and so does the description now. Neither is a fact about the world, so neither has a source; nothing else in either story changed.

## 5. Methods

| Method | Source | Read on |
|---|---|---|
| Internal and external factor evaluation (IFE, EFE): weights adding up to 1 on each side; ratings 1 to 4; 2.5 is average | Fred R. David, Forest R. David and Meredith E. David, Strategic Management: A Competitive Advantage Approach, Concepts and Cases, 18th edition, Pearson 2023 (Global Edition 2025, ISBN 9781292745275), read through excerpts of its chapter on strategy analysis and choice | 2026-11-23 |
| The IE matrix: IFE across and EFE up, each 1.0 to 1.99, 2.0 to 2.99 and 3.0 to 4.0; cells I, II and IV grow and build; III, V and VII hold and maintain; VI, VIII and IX harvest or divest | The same book | 2026-11-23 |
| The TOWS matrix: strategies that pair strengths or weaknesses with opportunities or threats (SO, WO, ST, WT) | Heinz Weihrich, "The TOWS Matrix: A Tool for Situational Analysis", Long Range Planning 15 (2), 54–66, 1982, https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-6301(82)90120-0 | 2026-11-23 |

One wording differs from the brief: David calls an external rating of 2 "average", where the brief says "below average". The tool follows David, as the plan decided (PLAN.md, section 15), and the Method dialog shows his scale.
